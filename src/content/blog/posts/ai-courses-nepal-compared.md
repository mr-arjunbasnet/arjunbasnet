Search for "AI course in Nepal" and you mostly find colleges promoting their own degree. That is understandable, but it is not much help if you are trying to work out which kind of course you actually need.

This is the map I wish existed: every serious route to learning AI in Nepal in 2026, from four-year degrees to free online courses to a one-day session in your own office, with who each one suits. I run AI training myself, so I have tried to be fair to the options that compete with mine. For most people reading this, the right answer is not my workshop, and I will say when.

Every fact below was checked against the provider's own page or published news on 25 October 2026. Programmes change; confirm dates, fees and entry rules with the provider before you apply.

## First, decide what you want AI for

The single most useful question is: **do you want to build AI, or use it?**

- **Building AI** means writing the software: training models, working with data, building AI products. That needs programming and mathematics, and the routes are degrees, fellowships and technical courses.
- **Using AI** means getting real work done with tools like ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Copilot: writing, research, analysis, reporting, teaching. That needs no coding, and the routes are short courses, online courses and workshops.

Most people searching for an AI course in Nepal want the second, and are shown the first. A marketing officer does not need a B.Tech. A school leaver who wants to work at an AI company probably does.

## The five routes at a glance

| Route | Typical length | Coding needed? | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| University degree | 3–4 years | Yes | School leavers who want a career building AI |
| Fellowship or AI school | 10 days to 6 months | Yes | Students and engineers with a technical background, selected competitively |
| Short course at a training institute | A few weeks to 6 months | Usually not | Individuals who want structured, classroom-based learning |
| International online course | 4 hours to 7 weeks | Depends on the course | Self-learners anywhere in Nepal, often free |
| Workshop inside your organisation | 1–3 days | No | Teams that need to use AI on their own work, safely |

## Route 1: university degrees in AI

Nepal now has a small but real set of AI degrees. These suit school leavers and graduates who want to build AI systems for a living.

| Institution | Programme | Length | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kathmandu University, Department of AI | B.Tech and M.Tech in Artificial Intelligence | 4 years (B.Tech) | Launched in 2021 as Nepal's first AI degrees; in-person at Dhulikhel |
| Kantipur City College (Purbanchal University) | B.Tech in AI | 4 years | Launched 2025; +2 Science and the PU entrance exam |
| King's College, Babarmahal | Master of Science in AI (MSAI) | 2 years, 60 credits | Linked to Westcliff University, USA; confirm the exact degree title with the college |
| Tribhuvan University, School of Mathematical Sciences | Bachelor of Data Science | 4 years | Entrance exam and interview; the 2024 intake had 40 seats |
| Kathmandu University, School of Science | Bachelor of Data Science | 4 years | Started 2024; admission through KUCAT |
| The British College, Thapathali (UWE Bristol) | BSc (Hons) Computer Science – Artificial Intelligence | 3 years (4 with foundation) | UK degree taught in Kathmandu; IELTS required |
| Islington College (London Metropolitan University) | BSc (Hons) Computing with Artificial Intelligence | 3 years | UK degree taught in Kathmandu |

A data science degree is not the same as an AI degree, but the two overlap heavily, and data science graduates often end up in AI roles. If the specific word "AI" on the certificate matters less to you than the skills, it is worth comparing both.

**Choose this route if** you are at the start of your career, you enjoy mathematics and programming, and you want to build AI rather than use it. **Skip it if** you already have a career and want AI to make you better at it. Four years is the wrong answer to that question.

## Route 2: fellowships and AI schools

These are intensive, selective and technical. They are some of the best-value AI education in Nepal, because they are free or low-cost to those selected.

- **Fusemachines AI Fellowship.** A six-month hybrid programme (online, with weekly live lectures and onsite sessions). The 2026 Nepal cohort was announced in February 2026, the programme's ninth year. It is a full scholarship if selected, with multi-stage selection that favours a programming, maths or computer science background.
- **NAAMII Annual Nepal AI School (ANAIS).** The 2026 edition runs from 28 December 2026 to 7 January 2027: ten days of lectures and labs, fully onsite. It is aimed at students and professionals with a computer science, engineering, maths, AI or data background. When I checked, the Nepal scholarship deadline was 31 October 2026 and the regular deadline 15 November 2026.

**Choose this route if** you already code and want depth, a cohort and a credential that the Nepali AI community recognises. **Skip it if** you are non-technical. You will not be selected, and it is not designed for you.

## Route 3: short courses at Kathmandu training institutes

Kathmandu's IT training institutes have added AI to their catalogues quickly. These suit people who want a structured course, a classroom and a teacher, without a degree.

| Provider | Course | Length | Format |
|---|---|---|---|
| Broadway Infosys, Tinkune | AI for Everyone (36 hours); also Prompt Engineering, AI for Business Leaders & Managers, Agentic AI, Python with AI | About 1 month for AI for Everyone; others from 16 days to 3.5 months | Physical or online live; AI for Everyone needs only basic computer skills |
| Skill Shikshya, Sankhamul | Generative AI Job Ready Program | 6 months | Hybrid; no prior experience required |
| Frontline Consult | AI Training in Nepal: Enhance Productivity | 7 hours (1 day) | Hands-on workshop |

Fees are not consistently published, so ask each provider directly and compare what is included: hours of live teaching, class size, whether you work on a project of your own, and what support you get afterwards.

**How to judge a short course:** ask for the syllabus hour by hour. A real AI course spends most of its time on hands-on work with current tools, teaches you how to check AI output, and covers what you must never paste into an AI tool. A weak one is mostly slides about the history of AI, or a general computer course with "AI" added to the title.

## Route 4: international online courses, many of them free

Some of the best AI courses in the world can be taken from Nepal at no cost. For self-motivated learners, this is where I would start.

| Course | Provider | Length | Cost | Coding needed? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Elements of AI | University of Helsinki and MinnaLearn | Self-paced | Free | Part 1 no; Part 2 basic Python |
| AI For Everyone | DeepLearning.AI on Coursera (Andrew Ng) | About 7 hours | Free to audit; financial aid available | No |
| AI Fluency: Framework & Foundations | Anthropic Academy | About 4 hours | Free | No |
| Google AI Essentials | Google on Coursera | Under 5 hours | US$49 a month after a 7-day trial (US price; may be lower elsewhere) | No |
| CS50's Introduction to AI with Python | Harvard (OpenCourseWare) | 7 weeks of material | Free; paid certificates optional | Yes, about a year of Python |

### The payment problem

Paid online courses are priced in US dollars, and paying from Nepal is not always simple. Nepal Rastra Bank allows banks to issue prepaid dollar cards for online payments. The online spending cap has been reported at **USD 500 a year** (Onlinekhabar, March 2026), which a few course subscriptions can use up. Check the current limit with your bank before you commit. For many learners this is one more reason to start with the free options above.

**Choose this route if** you are disciplined about finishing things on your own, or you live outside Kathmandu. **Its weakness** is that nobody helps you apply what you learn to your own work, and completion rates for self-paced courses are notoriously low.

## Route 5: a workshop inside your organisation

This is the route I deliver, so weigh what follows with that in mind.

An in-house workshop suits a **team**, not an individual. It is the right choice when several people in one organisation need to change how they work, and the training has to fit your tools, your data rules and your tasks. A one-day session for 8 to 30 people typically covers:

- safe use: what must never go into an AI tool;
- technique applied to the team's real work;
- checking AI output, including in Nepali;
- one workflow per person that they keep using afterwards.

The full hour-by-hour version is published in [AI training for employees in Nepal: a sample one-day syllabus](/blog/ai-training-syllabus-nepal).

**Choose this route if** you are an HR manager, principal or business owner and the goal is a team that uses AI well. **Skip it if** only one or two people need general AI literacy. A free online course is better value, and I would tell you so. If you are comparing trainers, the [nine questions to ask an AI trainer](/blog/choose-ai-trainer-nepal) will help.

I also run individual and small-group courses, starting with [AI Fundamentals](/ai-courses/ai-fundamentals) for beginners. All of them are listed on the [AI courses in Nepal](/ai-courses-nepal) page.

## Which route should you choose?

| You are… | Start with | Then consider |
|---|---|---|
| A +2 student who wants to work in AI | A university AI or data science degree | Fusemachines Fellowship or ANAIS once you can code |
| A university student in computing | CS50 AI (free) | ANAIS, the Fusemachines Fellowship |
| A working professional, non-technical | Elements of AI or AI For Everyone (free) | A hands-on short course or workshop on your own tasks |
| A teacher | Anthropic's AI Fluency or Elements of AI (free) | A school workshop covering classroom rules and assessment |
| A manager or business owner | A half-day leadership session | A team workshop once tools and rules are decided |
| An HR or L&D manager booking for a team | An in-house workshop with a pre-assessment | Follow-up sessions for teams that will automate work |

## Five mistakes to avoid

1. **Paying for what is free.** Check whether a free course covers the same ground before you pay for an introductory one.
2. **Choosing by certificate.** Outside degrees, a certificate mostly proves attendance. Choose courses that end with something you built.
3. **Learning a tool your organisation will not let you use.** If you are learning for work, find out which tools and accounts your employer allows first.
4. **Skipping safe use.** Any course that never mentions what you must not paste into an AI tool is incomplete. This matters even more in workplaces, where, as [a recent survey of 23 Nepali team leaders](/blog/ai-training-pre-assessment-nepal) showed, staff often adopt AI before any rule exists.
5. **Taking a degree to answer a workplace question.** If the goal is to do your current job better, four years is the wrong tool.
